# Business Plan Review Checklist

Use this checklist only when a full-plan review, competition submission review, or final pre-submission audit is requested.

## 1. Executive summary
- Can the project be understood in 30 seconds?
- Are target customer, problem, solution, differentiated mechanism, evidence, and commercial path visible early?
- Does the summary lead with the commercial contradiction rather than generic macro background?
- Is the strongest result or traction point included?

## 2. Problem
- Is the pain specific, frequent, costly, risky, or strategically important?
- Is the affected customer clearly identified?
- Is the cost of the problem quantified when evidence exists?
- Does the plan distinguish a real buying problem from a minor inconvenience?

## 3. Existing alternatives
- What do customers use today?
- Why is the current approach insufficient?
- Is the gap important enough to create willingness to change or pay?
- Is the critique specific rather than dismissive?

## 4. Market
- Are TAM, SAM, and SOM separated?
- Are bottom-up estimates used where possible?
- Does the plan show a realistic first customer segment?
- Are market sources and dates credible?
- Is "large industry = project will succeed" avoided?

## 5. Product
- Is the product explained through a user workflow rather than a feature inventory?
- Does each major capability solve a concrete customer problem?
- Is the before/after difference clear?
- Is the product scope appropriate to the current maturity stage?

## 6. Technology and moat
- What technical mechanism matters commercially?
- Why is it difficult to replicate?
- Is the moat technological, data-driven, workflow-based, channel-based, supply-based, regulatory, brand-based, or network-based?
- Does the plan avoid calling ordinary implementation choices "core technology"?

## 7. Competition
- Are real alternatives included, including manual work and "do nothing"?
- Are comparison dimensions relevant to purchasing decisions?
- Does the plan avoid claiming superiority on every dimension?
- Is the chosen battlefield one where the project has evidence-backed advantage?

## 8. Validation
- Separate problem validation, demand validation, product validation, paid validation, replication validation, and growth validation.
- Label pilots, LOIs, free trials, paid contracts, and revenue accurately.
- Put the strongest real-world evidence first.

## 9. Business model
- Who pays?
- What triggers payment?
- What is the pricing unit?
- What is average contract/order value?
- What are delivery and support costs?
- What drives gross margin and recurrence?
- Is the main revenue engine clear?

## 10. Go-to-market
- Where do the first 10 customers come from?
- What channel acquires them?
- Who owns the buying decision?
- What is the expected sales cycle?
- What changes when expanding from 10 to 100 to 1,000 customers?

## 11. Financials
- Can revenue be reconstructed from customer count x price?
- Are cost assumptions explicit?
- Are gross margin and cash requirements plausible?
- Is break-even logic shown?
- Does growth follow an operational mechanism rather than wishful extrapolation?

## 12. Team
- Does each member's role connect to a critical execution need?
- Are awards included only when they strengthen credibility?
- Are missing capabilities acknowledged through hiring/advisor/partner plans when material?

## 13. Roadmap and use of funds
- Does each milestone reduce a key uncertainty?
- Is funding tied to specific capability, customer, product, regulatory, or scale milestones?
- Does the plan show what becomes possible after the resource is deployed?

## 14. Risk
- Focus on risks that can change the commercial conclusion.
- For each risk, state trigger, impact, current control, and next mitigation.
- Avoid generic lists of every possible problem.
- Do not suppress legal, safety, compliance, financial, or customer-concentration risks when material.

## 15. Final line-by-line audit
For each paragraph, ask:
- What decision does this paragraph support?
- Is the claim precise?
- Is the evidence visible or traceable?
- Is there unnecessary self-weakening?
- Is there empty hype?
- Can the paragraph be shortened without losing proof?
